Question: A 2000 kg truck requires 8000 N to accelerate. Find acceleration. Answer: a = 8000/2000 = 4 m/s².
Question: State the second law for angular motion. Answer: τ = Iα.
Question: A 0.15 kg baseball is hit with 300 N. Find acceleration. Answer: a = 300/0.15 = 2000 m/s².
Question: If net force is zero, the second law predicts? Answer: It predicts zero acceleration.
Question: A 50 kg crate is pulled with 200 N at 30°. Friction 50 N. Find acceleration. Answer: F_x = 200 cos30° = 173.2 N, Net = 173.2 - 50 = 123.2 N, a = 123.2/50 = 2.464 m/s².
Question: What is the differential equation of the second law? Answer: F = m d²x/dt².
Question: A 0.008 kg bullet accelerates at 10000 m/s². Find force. Answer: F = 0.008 × 10000 = 80 N.
Question: If m = 15 kg and a = 2.5 m/s², F = ? Answer: F = 15 × 2.5 = 37.5 N.
Question: A 75 kg skydiver reaches terminal velocity. Net force? Answer: Net force = 0 at terminal velocity.
Question: How does the second law define inertial mass? Answer: Inertial mass is the ratio of net force to acceleration: m = F/a.
Question: A 0.3 kg ball is thrown with 45 N. Find acceleration. Answer: a = 45/0.3 = 150 m/s².
Question: If F = 60 N and a = 12 m/s², m = ? Answer: m = 60/12 = 5 kg.
Question: A 400 kg motorcycle accelerates at 5 m/s². Find force. Answer: F = 400 × 5 = 2000 N.
Question: What is the second law's connection to conservation of momentum? Answer: If net external force is zero, momentum is conserved (dp/dt = 0).
Question: A 0.02 kg dart accelerates at 300 m/s². Find force. Answer: F = 0.02 × 300 = 6 N.
Question: If a = 9.8 m/s² and m = 10 kg, F = ? Answer: F = 10 × 9.8 = 98 N.
Question: A 1800 kg truck has engine force 5400 N. Find acceleration. Answer: a = 5400/1800 = 3 m/s².
Question: State the second law in Einstein's notation. Answer: F^μ = dp^μ/dτ, where p^μ is four-momentum.
Question: A 0.25 kg hockey puck is hit with 75 N. Find acceleration. Answer: a = 75/0.25 = 300 m/s².
Question: If F = 8 N and m = 2 kg, a = ? Answer: a = 8/2 = 4 m/s².
Question: A 70 kg person in free fall. Air resistance 200 N. Find acceleration. Answer: Net = (70×9.8) - 200 = 686 - 200 = 486 N, a = 486/70 ≈ 6.94 m/s².
Question: What is the correct interpretation of F = ma? Answer: Net force equals mass times acceleration; this is valid for constant mass in inertial frames.
Question: A 1000 kg satellite in orbit experiences 2000 N thrust. Find acceleration. Answer: a = 2000/1000 = 2 m/s².
Question: How does acceleration change if force is applied at an angle? Answer: Only the component of force in the direction of motion causes acceleration in that direction.
Question: A 2.5 kg object has forces 15 N north and 20 N east. Find acceleration magnitude. Answer: Net = √(15²+20²)=25 N, a=25/2.5=10 m/s².
Question: What does F = ma imply about objects with larger mass? Answer: They require larger force to achieve the same acceleration.
Question: A 0.12 kg baseball is thrown at 40 m/s. The catcher stops it in 0.05 s. Find average force. Answer: a = 40/0.05 = 800 m/s², F = 0.12 × 800 = 96 N.
Question: If a car doubles its speed, how does braking force relate? Answer: Stopping distance quadruples, but force depends on deceleration: F = m × (v²/(2d)).
Question: A 85 kg person rides an elevator accelerating upward at 1.5 m/s². Find normal force. Answer: N = m(g+a) = 85 × (9.8+1.5) = 960.5 N.
Question: What is the relationship between the second law and free-body diagrams? Answer: Free-body diagrams show all forces; their vector sum equals m×a.
Question: A 3 kg block is pulled with 12 N at 40° above horizontal. Find horizontal acceleration. Answer: F_x = 12 cos40° = 9.19 N, a = 9.19/3 = 3.06 m/s².
Question: If net force is 0, can an object be moving? Answer: Yes, it can move with constant velocity.
Question: Summarize Newton's second law in one sentence. Answer: The net force acting on an object equals the product of its mass and its acceleration, with both force and acceleration having the same direction.
